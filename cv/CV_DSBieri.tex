\subsection*{Profile}
\noindent Urban and real estate economist working where money, credit and property markets meet, at the intersection of real estate and housing finance, regional economics, public finance and the history of economic thought. The unifying claim of my research is that the spatial economy is a monetary economy: how money, credit and regulation move across space explains why places grow, stall and lock in. Three strands carry this programme -- real estate and housing finance and the geography of money, including the regulation of housing credit and of new financial markets; financial lock-in and regional persistence, from the Basel Process to the American South; and the German tradition of spatial economics, anchored in a decade of archival work on August L\"{o}sch that has produced a 1,100-page critical edition of his correspondence (Metropolis, 2026) and a volume of translated essays under contract with Springer. Before the academy I spent seven years at the Bank for International Settlements, latterly as Adviser to the General Manager. I have supervised or served on the committees of fifteen completed doctoral dissertations, two postdoctoral fellows and eight master's theses; taught more than 1,750 students since 2017, more than 1,600 of them in Principles of Real Estate; and am a frequent voice on housing, the Fed and the economy in national and international media (273 appearances since 2014).
