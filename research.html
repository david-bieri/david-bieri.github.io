Research

One question runs through everything: the spatial economy is a monetary economy, and the contemporary canon of regional economics has forgotten this. The programme works that question from four directions — theory, regional history, the history of ideas, and the plumbing of international finance.

Real estate finance, credit and urban form

Housing finance · property markets · spatial equilibrium

Real estate finance is where macroeconomic, spatial and micro-level forces all leave observable, identifiable traces. The work here connects the Rosen–Roback spatial equilibrium to the Leontief price dual — arguing that the firm-side zero-profit condition of spatial equilibrium is the input–output price system, and that financial instability shows up as a systematic deviation from that locus. Earlier pieces treat crowdfunding and "cataclysmic money" in city finance, the regulation of housing credit, and the national value of local amenities. Its housing-finance policy strand, the GSE reform trilemma, is set out under financial lock-in below.

Key outputs: Journal of Environmental Economics and Management (2023, with Kuminoff and Pope); Environment and Planning A (2015); Housing Policy Debate (2017); Moonlights, Sunspots and Frontier Finance (2014). Textbook in preparation: The Credibility of Place.

Financial lock-in and regional persistence

Evolutionary economic geography · regulation · public finance

Regions carry their financial history. Applying Schumpeter's Gesetz–Gestalt dialectic and the tools of evolutionary economic geography, this strand traces the conduits through which regulation produces path dependence: how the American South was locked in financially twice, antebellum and post-war; how the Basel Process created a new geography of regulation; and why housing-finance reform in the United States is caught in a trilemma whose dynamics an agent-based model reveals as a temporal trap.

Key outputs: Cambridge Journal of Regions, Economy and Society (2009; 2024; "Carriers of History", in revision); GSE Reform Trilemma manuscript for Socio-Economic Review.

August Lösch and German spatial economics

History of economic thought · archives · digital humanities

August Lösch (1906–1945) founded modern spatial economics and, almost alone among its founders, insisted that money has a geography. The critical edition of his correspondence — 450 letters, 1926–1945, with apparatus, indices and genealogies — appeared with Metropolis Verlag in 2026 after more than a decade of archival work. It anchors a broader project on the transatlantic transmission of German Konjunkturforschung and location theory into American regionalism and New Deal reform, with the South as laboratory, and their re-export to postwar Europe. Research stays in 2026–27: UNC Chapel Hill, Duke, the Rockefeller Archive Center and the Kiel Institute.

Key outputs: August Lösch: Briefe / Letters (Metropolis, 2026); "Back to the Future: Lösch, Isard, and the Role of Money and Credit in the Space-Economy" (Springer, 2017); august-loesch.org. Sabbatical book: From the New South to the New Europe.

The institutional architecture of international money

Reserve management · central banking · geoeconomics

Seven years at the Bank for International Settlements left a lasting interest in how the international monetary system holds together: reserve-currency dynamics, correspondent-banking architecture, the design of financial sanctions, and the de facto and personal independence of central banks. This strand feeds directly into public commentary on the Federal Reserve, tariffs and the dollar, and into GECKO, an initiative on geoeconomic wargaming that joins qualitative scenario work with quantitative modelling and simulation.

Key outputs: "The Basel Process and Financial Stability" (2004); "Financial Stability, the Basel Process and the New Geography of Regulation" (CJRES, 2009); commentary archive under In the news.
